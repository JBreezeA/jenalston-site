Rankings tell you where a page sits in Google. They don't tell you whether ChatGPT recommends you, whether Gemini names a competitor instead, or whether Google's AI Overview cites your product page or someone else's.

**AI share of voice** answers that question. It's the metric I rely on most when running GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization) programs, and you can start measuring it today with nothing more than a spreadsheet.

## What AI share of voice measures

AI share of voice is the percentage of brand mentions in AI-generated answers that belong to you. If an AI answer about design tools names five brands and you're one of them, you hold part of that answer. Across a set of prompts and platforms, those mentions add up to a share you can track over time.

It sits alongside two related signals, and it's worth recording all three separately:

- **Mention:** The AI names your brand or product in its answer.
- **Citation:** The AI lists one of your pages as a source.
- **Link:** The answer links directly to the page you're trying to promote.

A mention builds awareness. A citation and a link are what send people to your site.

## The formula

> AI share of voice = (your brand's mentions ÷ total mentions of all tracked brands) × 100

"All tracked brands" means you plus the competitors you've chosen to watch. Pick them once and keep the list stable, or your numbers won't be comparable month to month.

## A worked example

Say you market a design app and you're tracking four competitors. You run five prompts on three AI platforms and count every brand mention in the answers.

| Platform | Your mentions | All brand mentions | AI share of voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews | 8 | 20 | 40% |
| ChatGPT | 5 | 16 | 31% |
| Gemini | 3 | 15 | 20% |
| **All platforms** | **16** | **51** | **31%** |

The blended number, 31%, is useful for trend reports. The per-platform numbers are where the decisions are. In this example you're strong in AI Overviews and weak in Gemini, so Gemini is where the next round of work should focus.

## How to run the test, step by step

### 1. Build a focused prompt set

Start with the questions your content answers, the questions customers actually ask, and the follow-up questions AI tools generate on their own (often called query fan-outs). In my testing, a tight set of five prompts produced more relevant and consistent results than ten, so start small and go deep.

### 2. Choose your platforms

Track Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity at minimum. They disagree with each other more often than you'd expect: I've seen the same prompt earn strong brand visibility in ChatGPT and none at all in Gemini.

### 3. Keep conditions consistent

Use a fresh chat for every prompt, test in a private or logged-out window where you can, and run each round from the same location within the same week. AI answers shift with context, so consistency is what makes your numbers comparable.

### 4. Record more than mentions

For every answer, log whether your brand was mentioned, cited and linked, its position in the answer, which competitors appeared, and the overall sentiment. A spreadsheet with one tab per platform keeps this manageable. It's the same structure as [the tracker I built for an enterprise GEO program](/case-studies#enterprise-geo).

### 5. Ask the follow-up question

Many first answers explain a topic without recommending any tools. Adding a follow-up such as "Include the top tools to use" often surfaces brand mentions that weren't in the first response. Record follow-up results in their own column so you can report both.

### 6. Track over time

Re-run the same prompts monthly, and after every major content launch. AI answers drift as models and sources change, and the trend line is what tells you whether your work is moving the needle.

## Mistakes that skew the number

- **Counting branded prompts as wins.** If a prompt includes your brand name, the AI will almost always mention you. Track branded prompts separately, or label them clearly in your reports.
- **Treating one run as a result.** The same prompt can return different answers minutes apart. Run each prompt two or three times and average the results.
- **Blending mentions and links.** A mention without a link still matters, but it doesn't drive traffic the way a citation does. Report them separately.
- **Reporting only the blended number.** A healthy overall share can hide a platform where you're invisible.

## Tools that help

Manual tracking in a spreadsheet works well, especially at the start. AI-visibility tools such as Semrush's AI visibility toolkit, Ahrefs Brand Radar and HubSpot's AEO Grader can speed up discovery and add scale. Whichever you use, spot-check its results by running a few prompts yourself. In my experience, a quick manual check is the fastest way to confirm a tool is reading the right signals.

## What to do with your number

If your share is low on a platform, look at the sources that platform cites in its answers. Those are the pages shaping the AI's view of your category, and getting your brand featured accurately on them is often the fastest way in. Keep your brand name close to the topics you want to own, in your own content and in [listings](/case-studies#directory-listings) and articles elsewhere. Then measure again.
